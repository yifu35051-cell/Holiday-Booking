WeChat Pay's blocker matters for your report. Multiple sources confirm the sandbox
itself has no real-name requirement, but it validates against the production merchant
account's real-name status — return MCH_NOT_REALNAME if unverified. As an
individual developer in Chengdu you cannot get a           商户号. So either use Alipay alone,
or document WeChat Pay as a designed-but-unimplemented adapter — which is a
legitimate architectural exercise and demonstrates your port/abstraction layer working.

Three caveats specific to your position

1. ICP 备案     applies to production, not to your project. The moment you serve a
public site from a mainland cloud, the domain needs ICP             备案     and the database must
be with the same provider as the filer. Irrelevant for coursework — but if §2.6 lists a
deployment target, mention it.

2. Production payments are out of reach as an individual. Alipay and WeChat Pay
both require    营业执照        for live accounts. Your SRS/OOD should state this explicitly: the
payment integration is designed and sandbox-verified, not production-ready. That's an
honest scope boundary, and stating it earns more credit than omitting it.

3. Free-tier numbers here are less stable than Western equivalents. SendCloud's
free quota differs across its own pricing page, FAQ, and docs; 腾讯云                  SES overage is

integrate and record the date in §4.1 of your OOD report.
